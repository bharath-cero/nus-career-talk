## 1. Career map vs career ladder — LinkedIn, 2022

`linkedin.com/posts/activity-7001469021552328704-53Tz`

The origin of the framing, and the source for Act I. **He did not coin it** — he
credits Jason Shah on Lenny Rachitsky's podcast, episode *"Building a meaningful
career."* QR on slide 5 points at the newsletter version, which carries the full
transcript: `lennysnewsletter.com/p/building-a-meaningful-career-jason`

Jason Shah, quoted in the post and on the slide:

> "A lot of people think about their next job, their next title, how much
> salary… but they're very unintentional about the macro. What do I care about as
> an individual? Where do you get energy from? … I found that really clarifying
> and it makes career decisions that have seemed risky to other people seem
> inevitable to me."

His own framing: *"changing four role profiles, six industries & three
geographies"*, and *"it has required a lot of hustle to manifest."*

**What the map bought him**, his list: a fulfilling and enriching career;
breadth of skills and methods; cross-industry awareness; **comfort and
confidence in ambiguity**; great work-related bar stories.

**What it cost him** — this is the honest beat on slide 6, and it is his own,
so don't soften it:

> "the real balancing act has been to traverse my career map while not
> completely abandoning hopes of also climbing the ladder … 1) you have to
> regularly re-establish your reputation, and 2) great opportunities find you
> less often. You can mitigate some of this (i.e. hustle and owning your story),
> but it can get quite taxing."

His actual advice is **not** map-over-ladder. It is: *"think about what really
matters to you keeping the ladder vs. map paradigm in mind, and see how best you
could optimize for both."*

## 2. Future of analytics with AI — LinkedIn, ~3 months ago

`linkedin.com/posts/kbharath87_for-years-at-airwallex-weve-split-data-…`

Commentary on a post by Timothy Wong (Airwallex). **For Act III.**

Timothy Wong's line, which he singles out:

> "Easy access to analysis doesn't translate into higher decision quality. It
> often does the opposite. More charts, more interpretations, more
> confidently-wrong narratives."

His own: *"analysis scales faster than rigor"*, and the job of a modern analytics
team is *"creating a shared language for how the company understands reality and
makes decisions"* — not throughput.

**Three ways he sees the craft evolving.** These double as three concrete career
paths for students:

1. **Analytics Engineering** — semantic layers, governed metrics, knowledge and
   context graphs, evals, golden datasets. (Term "Analytics Intelligence
   Engineering" credited to Hamzah Chaudhary.)
2. **Data Product Management** — canonical views built like products: fast,
   intuitive, genuinely useful. Measure usage, make sure the output lands.
3. **Strategic Storytelling & Influence** — consultative engagement, problem
   framing, judgement, communication. *"The bar just got higher."*

## 5. Movement of atoms — LinkedIn, ~3 weeks ago

`linkedin.com/posts/kbharath87_movementofatoms-fooddelivery-ai-…`

**For Act II.** His current thinking on the industry, and the strongest hook for
a Faculty of Science audience, because the bottleneck is physical.

> "food delivery is still the incredibly difficult task of reliably moving a hot
> meal from Party A, through Party B, to Party C, at massive scale. There are
> simply too many human points of failure. Restaurants get busy. Riders get
> delayed. Weather changes. Orders get packed incorrectly. Food gets spilt."

The marketplace absorbs the cost of those failures, on top of a thin-margin
business. Constraints suppress supply *and* act as *"a wet blanket on demand."*

Disarming personal admission worth putting on a slide verbatim:

> "Even as someone who works in the industry, I prefer the reliability of
> picking up something nearby most Sunday dinners."

Improving customer experience is *"work towards a local maximum"*; the global
maximum is reliable **"movement of atoms."** Three signals he cites: Unitree
Super Athlete AS2-W (16kg continuous payload), DoorDash's FAA drone approval,
Travis Kalanick out of stealth with Atoms (cloud kitchens meets robotics).
Frames AI's effect on knowledge work as the *"movement of bits"* by contrast.

## 6. CDAO Data Leadership Summit Singapore — LinkedIn, ~4 months ago

`linkedin.com/posts/kbharath87_cdaosummitsingapore-dataleadership-…`

**For Act II**, if the org story is wanted. Eighteen months ago, merging three
brands: *"patch existing data silos or rebuild from first principles. We chose
the latter."* Three pillars: a hybrid org with a dedicated Analytics Engineering
function; a unified Data Mart, semantic layer and function-specific knowledge
graphs to make the ecosystem *"agent-friendly"*; and agentic data products —
**Niki**, an LLM agent in Slack acting as the front office for analytics
requests (explicitly *not* pure text-to-SQL), and **AURA** for automated QA and
case summarisation. His tagline: **"from shipping dashboards to shipping
decisions."**

## 7. Digitale Welt article — July 2026

`digitaleweltmagazin.de/wem-gehoert-die-ki-heute-warum-sich-die-traditionelle-tech-hierarchie-veraendert/`

German, by him. The fullest statement of his AI-enterprise thesis. **For Act III.**

- *"Who owns AI internally?"* is largely a self-referential debate that distracts
  from the real problem.
- Consumers are already past chatbots — building personal agentic setups,
  connecting them to their accounts, granting them authority to act. Once an
  agent fulfils everyday requests it sits between the user and the marketplace:
  **structural disintermediation risk.** Discovery still matters; for repeat
  tasks the interface stops mattering and advantage moves to speed and precision.
- The demo-to-production gap is the real difficulty. Long-running agents are
  **trusted delegates**, not software: they need deterministic controls, complete
  audit trails, working rollback. Without governance, **trust plateaus at about
  80% — and a product leaders half-trust never ships.**
- **The centralisation trap:** a small central AI team focused on the
  highest-value work becomes the bottleneck; many small wins die in the
  prioritisation gap.
- **Forward-deployed builders:** push building to AI-capable people inside the
  functions, inside guardrails — budget caps, controlled access, approved tools —
  so the centre can stay on the hard problems.
- Quotes a senior Singapore minister demonstrating his own agentic setup for
  diplomatic work: *"You cannot effectively govern a technology you only get
  briefed on."* Strong local anchor for this room.
